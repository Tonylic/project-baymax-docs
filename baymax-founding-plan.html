01The short version

Baymax is the technology finance analyst a small company never hires. An agent connects to the bills, builds and maintains the cost model, closes the month, and explains the numbers in plain language.

  1. Apptio stops at about $80K a year. Its cheapest listed package is $80,040 a year for up to $25M of IT spend, sold by demo, with services reported at 30 to 50% of first-year cost. There is no self-serve tier. AWS Marketplace
  2. Small companies do not ask for TBM. They ask why the bill moved, what their gross margin is, and what to forecast. We found no demand for towers, run-versus-grow or chargeback below the mid-market. Our read
  3. Seeing spend is already free. Vantage is free up to $2,500 a month of tracked spend, Ramp gives away AI token tracking, and AWS shipped a free FinOps agent in June 2026. A dashboard or a chat box over cost data is no longer a product. Vantage Ramp SiliconANGLE
  4. The open ground is the join. No product we found combines cloud, AI, SaaS and people cost in one model and allocates it to product and customer for a small company. Each incumbent owns one cost pool.
  5. Start where cost threatens survival. AI products run at 45 to 53% gross margin against 80% or more for classic SaaS. AI-native companies spending $10K or more a month on cloud and AI have a margin problem today. ICONIQ
  6. Sell the work first. The business founder delivers the first reports by hand, using the agent as a tool, and engineering automates the repeated steps. Vendr reached $1M of annual revenue with 19 customers by selling an outcome. TechCrunch

Direction

Run a five-week interview sprint, pick the wedge on 18 December 2026, then spend eight weeks proving that design partners will pay. On 12 February 2027 we either have paying customers or we change track.

02The field in ten minutes

Direction

Speak finance to customers and FinOps to data. Keep TBM as the method inside the product and as the bridge upmarket later.

TBM is a method for turning the general ledger's view of technology spend into views that IT and the business can act on. It is stewarded by the TBM Council, which began as Apptio's customer advisory board. The current standard is Taxonomy 5.0 (June 2025), which added cloud, SaaS and AI structures. TBM Council

For an engineer, a TBM model is an allocation graph over financial data. Money enters as ledger lines, is classified into cost pools, moves through shared technology towers using drivers, lands on products and services, and is assigned to whoever consumes them. Showback, unit cost, budget versus actual and benchmarks are all reads of that one graph.

Where the labor goes

Apptio practitioners describe loading 25 to 90 files a month, a close that runs from business day 2 to day 8, and a reconciliation meeting with Finance. IBM and its partners sell data management, model configuration, validation and report building as services. That list is the agent's job description. IBM community Apptio services

Three disciplines are merging

  • FinOps started with cloud bills and now claims all technology spend. In the State of FinOps 2026 survey, 98% of teams manage AI spend (31% in 2024), 90% manage SaaS and 28% manage labor. FinOps Foundation
  • TBM started at the ledger and added cloud, SaaS and AI in version 5.0.
  • SaaS management is being absorbed into larger platforms (section 4).

FOCUS is the data standard to build on

FOCUS is an open billing-data schema from the FinOps Foundation. Version 1.4 (June 2026) covers cost and usage, commitments, invoices and billing periods. Version 1.5, which adds AI token billing, is due in December 2026. AWS, Microsoft, Google and Oracle export it. OpenAI, Anthropic and mainstream SaaS vendors do not, so for small companies the real sources remain card feeds, ledgers and provider usage APIs. FOCUS

Why now

  1. AI gave software companies a variable cost of goods again. That recreates, at 30 to 300 people, the cost accounting need TBM served in large IT departments. It now points at product margin.
  2. Billing data is standardizing. FOCUS, provider usage APIs and MCP servers shrink the connector moat incumbents built.
  3. Analysts are moving the bar. Forrester's June 2026 evaluation says a tool that only explains where the money went is already obsolete, and it rewards natural-language interaction and ease of adoption. Forrester
  4. The incumbent's AI stops at assistants. IBM's November 2026 release added an AI assistant, report summaries, a data-preparation assistant and MCP servers. Nothing in it builds or runs the model. Apptio
  5. The neighboring category is collapsing. Six of 15 SaaS-management platforms were acquired and one shut down between January 2025 and October 2026.

Counter-evidence

  • Under 1% of companies with fewer than 100 employees spend over $100K a year on AI, and 87% of AI spenders call their spend sustainable. Mercury
  • About half of new US businesses are gone within five years. BLS
  • Self-serve visibility sells for about 1% of tracked spend.

Most small companies have nothing worth managing. The ones that do are a minority, and we have to find them by spend.

06Product and MVP

Direction

Build the analyst. The MVP gives an AI-native company three things: a margin table by customer, a plain account of what changed, and answers that show their sources. The business founder reviews every output for the first partners.

The product idea: allocate with drivers

Provider bills do not say which customer caused a cost. Engineering-led tools solve this by instrumenting every request, which takes months. TBM practitioners solve it with allocation drivers: usage signals a company already has, such as requests, seats, events or tokens per API key, that split a shared cost in a defensible way.

The agent proposes the drivers, states how confident it is, and tightens the model as better data arrives. A founder gets margin by customer this week, at a stated precision. This is where the business founder's allocation experience becomes product. To test

The Brief

Weekly and at month end. Total technology spend by pool, what changed and why, and three actions ranked by dollars. Sent to Slack and email.

The margin table

Revenue, allocated cost and gross margin by customer, plan and product. Each line names its driver and carries a confidence note.

Ask

Questions in plain language. Every number comes from a stored query and links to the rows behind it.

The concierge loop

  1. The customer grants read-only access.
  2. The agent ingests, classifies and drafts the report.
  3. The business founder corrects the draft and adds judgment.
  4. Each correction becomes a rule, a test case or a prompt change.

The first ten reports are slow on purpose. They produce the labeled data and the evaluation set.

What makes it hard to copy

  • A library of allocation drivers and classification rules that improves with every customer
  • Business context held over time: rules, contracts, renewals, decisions
  • Numbers that reconcile to invoices, with an audit trail
  • The first spend and margin benchmarks for companies under $50M in revenue

How we will know the MVP works

  • First report within 48 hours of access
  • Totals within 1% of provider invoices
  • Each partner makes one decision a month from the output
  • A founder or finance lead opens the Brief every week

07Technical architecture

Direction

Own the data. Ingest bills into a FOCUS-shaped store, define metrics in code, and let the agent produce numbers only through tools. Use MCP at the edges.

Five rules

  1. The model never does arithmetic. Every figure is the result of a tool call with a stored query.
  2. Metrics first, free SQL second. A small vendor-run benchmark in April 2026 put a semantic layer at 98 to 100% on in-scope questions, against 84 to 90% for text-to-SQL, and it fails loudly instead of silently. dbt
  3. Rules are data. Classification and allocation rules are versioned records that the agent proposes and a person approves.
  4. Reconcile or flag. Totals match the provider invoice, or the report says they do not.
  5. Read-only until trust is earned. Actions start as recommendations with evidence.

What code does and what the model does

TaskApproach
Classify cloud line itemsA rules table that the business founder authors once. No model call.
Classify new vendorsA small model, limited to our categories, with a confirmation queue. Vendor-run benchmarks put first-pass ledger categorization at roughly 66 to 87%, so design for fast correction.
Allocate shared costA deterministic engine that preserves totals. The model proposes rules and explains them.
Explain a varianceCode computes the breakdown: price, volume, mix, new vendors, credits. The model writes it up with links to rows.
Detect anomalies and forecastStatistics. The model triages and explains.

Stack for one engineer

  • Postgres for tenants, rules, connector state and logs
  • Per-customer Parquet files queried with DuckDB, so agent SQL can only see one customer's data
  • Python connectors with a shared contract
  • Our own agent loop on a provider SDK, with prompt caching and model routing by task

Costs to watch Estimates

  • Infrastructure for the first 50 customers: $100 to $500 a month
  • Model cost: about $0.09 to $0.18 per multi-step question, so cap questions on low tiers
  • Each connector is a permanent maintenance cost. Hold the MVP to five.

Security from day one

  • AWS access through an assumed role with an external ID. No long-lived keys. Datadog guidance
  • Customer secrets in a managed vault, never in prompts or logs.
  • Aggregate before the model sees data. People cost only at team level.
  • Treat ingested text such as tags, memos and vendor names as data that may carry instructions.
  • A public security page before the first partner. SOC 2 Type 1 around month six, when a mid-size prospect asks. Vanta
  • If we promise zero retention at the model provider, check which API features and model tiers that rules out.

Traps the research found

  • Credits hide cost. Startups on credits see near-zero bills. Show billed cost and pre-credit cost side by side.
  • Org-wide AWS data needs the payer account. A reseller-owned organization yields only member-account data.
  • Billing data is restated. Reprocess whole billing periods instead of appending.
  • API terms change. Intuit and Xero both began charging developers in 2025 to 2026.

09Pricing

Direction

Flat monthly tiers by total technology spend under management, plus a free one-time report. Price the analyst's work. Avoid per-seat pricing and a share of savings.

What the market has taught buyers to expect

  • Vantage: free to $2,500 a month of tracked spend, then $30 and $200 a month.
  • Cloudchipr: $49, $189 and $890 a month by spend band.
  • Ramp's AI spend tool, the AWS and Google agents, and Pump: free to the customer.
  • Cloudability from $30,000 a year. Apptio from $80,040.

Visibility is worth about 1% of tracked spend. Anything above that has to be justified by work delivered or margin recovered.
